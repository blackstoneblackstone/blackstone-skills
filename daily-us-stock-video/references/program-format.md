# Program Format

Use 6–9 scenes totaling 5–7 minutes.

| Section | Typical duration | Visual |
|---|---:|---|
| Cold open and market state | 30–50s | Market overview component |
| Main macro catalyst | 40–70s | Rebuilt macro graphic or privacy-safe Imagegen still |
| Data and risk calendar | 35–55s | Designed chart/calendar |
| Sector rotation | 30–50s | Market heatmap component |
| Stock or ETF 1 | 40–60s | Stock trend component |
| Stock or ETF 2 | 40–60s | Stock trend component |
| Additional stock/risk level | 30–50s | Stock trend component |
| Closing takeaway | 20–35s | Summary card |

Narration tone: informed, conversational, and consistently playful. Use one or two compact jokes per minute, plus callbacks where natural. Prefer a concrete setup and payoff over meme stacking. Avoid `不是……而是……`; state the observation and consequence directly.

Reserve a dedicated bottom caption band across every scene. Keep charts, axes, source labels, tickers, footers, disclosures, and decoration above it. The band may contain only the seamless global animated background when a caption is visible. Keep captions concise enough for two lines.

Place a 0.8–1.2 second pause between narration segments. Center the visual transition and SFX inside that pause, and keep the pause free of captions. Include these pauses when estimating the 5–7 minute total.

Use one local full-frame background image or texture as a seamless, deterministic animated loop spanning the root timeline. Scene cards and components may sit above it, but open areas should reveal the same continuous loop; never restart motion per scene.

For each scene record: scene ID, topic, ticker/component, narration text, measured audio duration, absolute start/end, subtitle cue range, source/provenance, and verification status. For each stock/ETF also record the source chart time grain, the evidence used to identify it, component interval, and data-snapshot interval.

For each boundary record: outgoing scene, incoming scene, pause start/end/duration, boundary type, visual transition, SFX ID/path, SFX start/duration/volume, generation or library provenance, and verification status. Use one coherent sound family across the program; vary the accent rather than changing sonic identity at every cut.
